# Human-Gold Annotation Guide: Real Spontaneous Audio (IndicVoices)

This document explains how to hand-annotate the **real spontaneous audio**
half of the human-gold pack — the subset pulled from IndicVoices rather than
generated by the benchmark. It is written for the annotator, and for anyone
reviewing the resulting labels before they go into the paper.

For the synthetic half of the pack (benchmark test sessions), see
`README.md` §3 — those clips validate the reference committee's excerpt
boundaries and follow a different process, not covered here.

---

## 1. Why this exists

The synthetic benchmark's silences are inserted by the experimenter, so real
conversational pause statistics, natural onsets, and natural offsets never
appear in it at all. This is the **only part of the pipeline** that tests VAD
against genuinely natural speech and pause structure — everything else
answers a narrower, cleaner, but synthetic question.

It exists specifically to counter the likeliest reviewer objection:
*"concatenated read speech isn't VAD."*

---

## 2. What arrives before you start

Running `python main.py --steps 03b` streams a small number of real
spontaneous clips per language directly from IndicVoices on HuggingFace (no
full corpus download — just enough to fill each language's budget, ~75
seconds by default). These land in:

```
03_benchmark/human_gold/real/
  real_hi_in_000.wav
  real_hi_in_000_TEMPLATE.txt   <- empty, waiting to be filled in
  real_hi_in_001.wav
  real_hi_in_001_TEMPLATE.txt
  ...
INSTRUCTIONS.txt                <- the generated guide, reproduced below
```

Each `_TEMPLATE.txt` is an **empty Audacity label file** — a placeholder, not
a starting hypothesis. There is no transcript and no automatic
pre-segmentation shown for these clips, unlike the synthetic half. There is
no known ground truth here at all: **your judgment is the ground truth.**

---

## 3. The annotation process

**Step 1 — Open in Audacity.**
Load the `.wav`, then `File > Import > Labels` on the matching
`_TEMPLATE.txt` to get an empty label track under the waveform.

**Step 2 — Listen and mark.**
Play through the clip. For every region that is speech, add a label spanning
from where speech starts to where it ends. Use both ear and eye together —
the waveform envelope helps place boundaries more precisely than either sense
alone.

**Step 3 — Apply the conventions** (below). These exist so the real-audio
labels use the *same* definition of "speech" as the synthetic benchmark,
making the two comparable.

**Step 4 — Export.**
`File > Export > Export Labels`, saving back over the same `_TEMPLATE.txt`
filename. That tab-separated file of `start_time  end_time` pairs is the
deliverable.

---

## 4. Conventions (must match the synthetic reference)

- **Pauses shorter than 0.20 s stay inside the speech region.** A stop
  closure or a geminate consonant's hold time is not silence — only longer
  gaps count as breaks between speech segments.
- **Breaths, coughs, lip smacks are not speech**, even though they are not
  silence either.
- **Boundaries go at the first/last actual acoustic evidence of speech** —
  the first glottal pulse or frication noise — not at the visual "knee" of
  the waveform envelope, which often lags the true onset.

---

## 5. Two annotators, independently

Every clip is labeled by **two people**, not one, and their outputs are kept
separate rather than merged or averaged. The disagreement itself is data: it
tells you how much boundary ambiguity is inherent to real speech even for
careful humans, and becomes the yardstick for judging whether a VAD system's
disagreement with either annotator is meaningful or within normal human
variation.

Disagreements are **not** resolved into a single consensus label. Report
Cohen's kappa and median boundary disagreement between the two annotators
instead.

---

## 6. Feeding results back into the pipeline

Once labels are exported over the `_TEMPLATE.txt` files, run:

```bash
python main.py --steps 03b --score-human-gold
```

This reads every returned label file, reconstructs each annotator's
segments, and writes `human_gold_summary.csv`. A `source` column
(`real_indicvoices` vs. `synthetic`) keeps the two halves separate — they
must **never** be pooled into one number, since they answer different
questions (external validity vs. reference-committee validation).

---

## 7. What this does and does not prove

**Does:** gives the only evidence in the pipeline about how these systems
perform on natural onsets, offsets, and pause structure in real South Asian
speech — not just on the carefully constructed synthetic proxy.

**Does not:** provide a large sample. ~75 seconds per language is enough for
a sanity check and an inter-annotator agreement number, not enough for a
statistically powered claim on its own. In the paper, present it as
supporting evidence for external validity, reported clearly separately from
the synthetic benchmark's headline numbers — exactly what the `source`
column is there to enforce.

## Appendix: exact text of the generated `INSTRUCTIONS.txt`

This is written automatically into `03_benchmark/human_gold/INSTRUCTIONS.txt`
by the pipeline (`steps/step08_phonology.py::make_human_gold_pack`), covering
both the synthetic and real halves together:

```
HUMAN GOLD ANNOTATION -- IndicVAD-Bench
=======================================

This pack has TWO parts, {n_synth} synthetic + {n_real} real clips:

  1. SYNTHETIC (top-level *.wav) -- exact-GT benchmark audio.
     Annotating these checks the reference-committee's excerpt
     boundaries, not real-world VAD performance.
  2. REAL (real/*.wav) -- spontaneous speech from IndicVoices
     (CC BY 4.0). No synthetic construction, no forced alignment:
     you are the ground truth. This is the external-validity
     evidence for the paper.

  [If applicable] NOTE: no real clips were obtained for: [...].
  Check network access and human_gold.real_source.lang_config_map
  against https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicVoices
  and re-run `python main.py --steps 03b`. The synthetic half
  for these languages is unaffected.

For each wav: open in Audacity, File > Import > Labels on the
matching _TEMPLATE.txt, mark every SPEECH region, then
File > Export > Export Labels back over the same filename.

Conventions (must match the synthetic reference):
  * Pauses SHORTER than 0.20 s are NOT marked as silence -- they
    stay inside the speech region. This covers stop closures and
    geminates, and is the convention the benchmark assumes.
  * Breaths, coughs, lip smacks: NOT speech.
  * Mark boundaries at the first/last visible glottal pulse or
    frication energy, not at the waveform envelope knee.
  * Two annotators per file. Disagreements are kept, not resolved:
    report Cohen's kappa and median boundary disagreement.
  * Report synthetic and real subsets SEPARATELY in the paper --
    they answer different questions (reference validation vs.
    external validity) and should not be pooled into one number.

Then run:  python main.py --steps 03b --score-human-gold
```
